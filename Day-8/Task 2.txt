1. User Query

What it does:
From my understanding, the User Query is the question that the user asks. It is the starting point of the RAG pipeline because the system first needs to know what information the user is looking for.

Why it is needed:
I think this step is important because without a question, the system will not know what to search for. A clear query helps the system retrieve more relevant information and generate a better answer.

2. Document Loading

What it does:
In this step, documents such as PDFs, Word files, websites, FAQs, and company documents are collected and loaded into the RAG system.

Why it is needed:
In my opinion, this step is necessary because the AI can only answer questions based on the documents that are available. If the required documents are not loaded, the system cannot use that information.

3. Text Chunking

What it does:
After learning about RAG, I understood that large documents are divided into smaller parts called chunks. Each chunk contains a small amount of meaningful information.

Example:
For example, a 100-page company policy document can be divided into many smaller paragraphs.

Why it is needed:
I feel this step is useful because LLMs cannot process very large documents at once. Splitting them into smaller chunks makes searching faster and improves the accuracy of the retrieved information.

4. Embeddings

What it does:
Embeddings convert text into numerical vectors that represent the meaning of the text instead of just the exact words.

Example:
"How do I apply for leave?"
"What is the leave application process?"

Even though these sentences use different words, they have the same meaning, so their embeddings will be very similar.

Why it is needed:
From what I learned, computers understand numbers much better than text. Embeddings help the system search based on the meaning of the question instead of only matching keywords.

5. Vector Database

What it does:
A Vector Database stores all the embeddings created from the document chunks. It helps the system quickly find information that is related to the user's question.

Examples:
• Pinecone
• ChromaDB
• FAISS
• Weaviate

Why it is needed:
I think this is an important part of RAG because searching through thousands of documents manually would take a lot of time. The vector database makes this process much faster and more efficient.

6. Similarity Search

What it does:
The user's question is also converted into an embedding. The system then compares it with the stored document embeddings and retrieves the most relevant chunks.

Why it is needed:
According to my understanding, similarity search focuses on the meaning of the question instead of exact words. Because of this, it can find better and more relevant information.

7. Large Language Model (LLM)

What it does:
The retrieved document chunks and the user's question are given to the LLM. The LLM reads the information and generates a clear and natural answer.

Why it is needed:
I believe the LLM is responsible for understanding the retrieved information and presenting it in a simple and easy-to-understand way instead of showing raw document content.

8. Final Response

What it does:
The Final Response is the answer generated by the LLM using both the retrieved document information and the user's question.

Why it is needed:
In my opinion, this is the most important step because it is the final answer that the user receives. Since the response is based on retrieved documents, it is usually more accurate and has a lower chance of giving incorrect information.
